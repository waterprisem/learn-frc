<WhyItMatters>
A motor reports how far it has turned in rotations. Your elevator needs meters, and your arm needs degrees.
If you tune against raw rotations, the numbers mean nothing, and a target of "1" could be a centimeter or a collision.
</WhyItMatters>

## Gear ratio

**Gear ratio** is the number of motor rotations it takes to turn the mechanism once. A ratio of 10 means 10 motor rotations per mechanism rotation, written 10:1.

A ratio above 1 is a **reduction**. It trades speed for torque (turning force):

- The mechanism turns slower by the ratio. At 10:1, a motor at 100 rotations per second (RPS) turns the mechanism at 10 RPS.
- The mechanism pushes harder by about the same ratio, before losses.

That is why an arm uses a big reduction and a flywheel uses almost none. The same trade holds for sprockets and pulleys: a small one driving a large one is a reduction.

## Motor rotations to meters

An elevator turns a drum (or sprocket) that pulls a belt. One full turn of the drum lifts the carriage by the drum's **circumference**, which is 2 × π × radius.

`meters = motor rotations ÷ gear ratio × circumference`

**Worked example.** The Lab 6 elevator has a ratio of 10 and a drum radius of 0.022 m.

1. Circumference = 2 × π × 0.022 m = 0.138 m per drum turn.
2. The motor turns 25 rotations, so the drum turns 25 ÷ 10 = 2.5 rotations.
3. Height = 2.5 × 0.138 m = **0.346 m**.

## Motor rotations to degrees

An arm's mechanism rotation is a turn of the arm itself, and one full turn is 360 degrees. There is no circumference.

`degrees = motor rotations ÷ gear ratio × 360`

**Worked example.** The Lab 7 arm has a ratio of 100.

1. The motor turns 30 rotations, so the arm turns 30 ÷ 100 = 0.3 rotations.
2. Angle = 0.3 × 360 = **108 degrees**.

<GoDeeper title="why the slide's formula looks different">
The Session 6 slide writes the elevator conversion as "rotations × circumference × gear ratio". That only works if the ratio is written as mechanism turns per motor turn (0.1 here).
The lab code and this lesson use motor turns per mechanism turn (10), so you divide. Always check which way a ratio is written before using it.
</GoDeeper>

## Checkpoint

<Checkpoint lesson="1.8">

<details>
<summary>What does a 10:1 reduction do to speed and to force?</summary>
The mechanism turns ten times slower than the motor and pushes about ten times harder, before friction and other losses.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Why does the arm conversion have no circumference?</summary>
The arm itself rotates, so one mechanism rotation is a full turn of 360 degrees. An elevator turns a drum into straight-line motion, so it needs the drum's circumference to turn rotations into meters.
</details>

</Checkpoint>

## Recap

<RecapCard>
- Gear ratio = motor rotations per mechanism rotation. A ratio above 1 trades speed for torque.
- Elevator: `meters = motor rotations ÷ ratio × 2πr`.
- Arm: `degrees = motor rotations ÷ ratio × 360`.
- Put the units in the names and convert once, so the rest of the code works in meters or degrees.
- Phoenix 6 can do the division in the motor controller with `withSensorToMechanismRatio`.
</RecapCard>
