<WhyItMatters>
PID waits for an error before it reacts, so it is always a little late. If you already know a wheel needs about 5 V to spin at 40 RPS,
you can send that voltage immediately. That prediction is feedforward, and it does most of the work in a velocity loop.
</WhyItMatters>

## Predict the voltage

Feedforward uses only the target. It never reads the encoder.

`voltage = kS + kV × target`

- **`kS`** (volts) is the small voltage that overcomes friction. Below it, the wheel does not move at all.
- **`kV`** (volts per RPS) is the extra voltage for each rotation per second of speed.

With `kS` = 0.2 V and `kV` = 0.12 V per RPS, a 40 RPS target gives 0.2 + 0.12 × 40 = 5.0 V.

Because feedforward acts before any error exists, it removes the gap that P alone left in lesson 3.3. PID is then left to fix only what the prediction missed, such as a sagging battery or a load.

Units matter. `kV` here is per RPS. A value measured per RPM would be off by a factor of 60.

## Experiment: find kS and kV

Everything starts at zero, so the wheel does not move. Use the "kS" and "kV" knobs, one at a time, with `kP` left at 0.

<FlywheelTuner client:visible guide={false} initialPreset="zero" />

1. Predict, then do: raise `kS` slowly with a target held. The wheel should stay still until `kS` passes a threshold. Which is the largest `kS` where it does not move? Use that value.
2. Predict, then do: hold 40 RPS and raise `kV`. Will the speed settle below, on, or above 40 RPS as `kV` grows? Find the value where it lands on the target.
3. Check 20 and 70 RPS. If `kV` is right, all three should land near their targets. If only 40 is right, which gain is wrong?

## Checkpoint

<Checkpoint lesson="3.4">

<details>
<summary>What does kS compensate for?</summary>
Static friction: the small voltage needed to get the wheel moving. It is a fixed number of volts, not scaled by speed.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Why does kV depend on the speed units?</summary>
It is volts per unit of speed. If the target is in RPS, kV is volts per RPS, and a value for RPM would be wrong by a factor of 60.
</details>

</Checkpoint>

## Recap

<RecapCard>
- Feedforward predicts voltage from the target alone: `kS + kV × target`.
- `kS` is volts to overcome friction. `kV` is volts per RPS.
- It acts before any error exists, so it removes most of what P alone could not.
- Find `kS` first, then `kV`, with `kP` at 0.
</RecapCard>
