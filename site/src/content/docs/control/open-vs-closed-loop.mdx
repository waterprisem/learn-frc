A shooter that sends 5 V to its wheel launches a note at one speed on a fresh battery and a slower speed on a tired one.
The shot lands short and nothing in the code knows. Control that checks the result fixes this.

## One voltage, many speeds

A motor's speed depends on more than the voltage you send it.

| What changes | Effect on the wheel at the same voltage |
|---|---|
| Battery sag (lesson 1.3) | The motor really gets less than you asked for, so it spins slower |
| A load, such as a game piece squeezed by the wheel | The wheel is dragged down |
| Friction that shifts with heat and wear | The wheel needs more voltage to hold the same speed |

So "set 5 V" is a request for a voltage, not for a speed. If you care about speed, you have to look at the speed.

## Two kinds of loop

An **open-loop** controller sends an output and never looks at the result. Setting a motor to a fixed voltage is open loop.

A **closed-loop** controller measures the result and uses it to change the output. Every 20 ms (50 times a second), it repeats three steps:

1. **Measure** the real speed with the encoder.
2. **Compare** it with the speed you want.
3. **Correct** the voltage to close the gap.

Open loop is fine when the result does not matter much, such as an intake that only has to spin. Use closed loop when the result matters, such as a shooter that must hit a speed.

## Checkpoint

<Checkpoint lesson="3.1">

<details>
<summary>Why does a fixed voltage not guarantee a fixed flywheel speed?</summary>
Battery voltage sags, friction changes, and loads such as a game piece drag the wheel down. The same voltage gives a different speed each time, and open-loop code never checks.
</details>

<details>
<summary>What makes a controller closed loop?</summary>
It uses a measurement of the result to change its output, repeatedly. Without the measurement, it is open loop.
</details>

</Checkpoint>

## Recap

<RecapCard>
- Voltage is not speed: battery sag, load, and friction all change the speed you get.
- Open loop never looks at the result. Closed loop does.
- A closed loop measures, compares, and corrects every 20 ms.
- Use closed loop when the result matters, such as shooter speed.
</RecapCard>
