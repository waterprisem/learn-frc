<WhyItMatters>
When a driver spins the robot to pick up a game piece, they should not have to think about which way the robot faces.
Field-relative driving makes "push away from me" always mean "robot moves away from me", and the code that does it is about 15 lines.
</WhyItMatters>

## Robot-relative versus field-relative

In **robot-relative** driving the stick is in the robot's frame: forward on the stick moves the robot toward its own front.
In **field-relative** driving the stick is in the driver's frame: forward moves the robot away from the driver, whichever way it faces. It needs the robot's heading, which comes from the Pigeon 2 gyro.

Say the stick is pushed forward and the robot faces left (+Y on the field).

| Mode | Robot moves |
| --- | --- |
| Robot-relative | Left (its own front), so it appears to go sideways to the driver |
| Field-relative | Away from the driver (+X), by steering the wheels at −90° in the robot frame |

If the gyro heading is wrong, field-relative drives in a rotated direction. A heading that is 90° off sends "forward" off to the side.

## Cleaning up the stick

A raw stick is not ready to drive with. Three steps fix that.

1. **Deadband.** A released stick may read 0.02 instead of 0. Treat anything under a small threshold as 0, or the robot creeps.
2. **Curve.** Raising the value to a power above 1 (the team uses 1.25) makes small pushes gentler, for fine aiming, while full push still reaches 1.
3. **Scale.** Multiply by the maximum speed, so full stick means the robot's top speed.

Team values: deadband 0.01, exponent 1.25, top speed 4.95 m/s, top turn rate one rotation per second (6.28 rad/s).

| Stick magnitude | After deadband and curve | Speed (m/s) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.005 | 0 | 0 |
| 0.5 | 0.415 | 2.06 |
| 1.0 | 1.0 | 4.95 |
