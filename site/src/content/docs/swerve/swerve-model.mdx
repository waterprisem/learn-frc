<WhyItMatters>
A tank drive can only go forward and turn. A swerve robot can slide sideways, drive diagonally, and spin while it does both, so it lines up with a scoring spot faster.
The price is four steered wheels that all have to agree. This lesson is the mental model that makes that manageable.
</WhyItMatters>

## One module

A swerve robot has four **modules**, one at each corner. Each module is a small steerable wheel with three devices:

- a **drive motor** that spins the wheel (speed),
- a **steer motor** that turns the whole wheel (direction),
- an **absolute encoder** that reports the wheel's angle even right after power-on.

<ComponentCard id="cancoder" />

The absolute encoder is a CANcoder (lesson 1.6). Without it the robot would not know where the wheels point at startup.
On the team's 2026 robot, front-left uses CAN ids 10 (drive), 11 (steer), and 12 (CANcoder), and a Pigeon 2 gyro sits at id 5.

## Ask for motion, not for wheels

Your code does not say "wheel 3, point left". It asks for one motion of the whole robot as a `ChassisSpeeds`, in the robot frame from lesson 6.1.

| Field | Meaning | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `vx` | forward speed | m/s |
| `vy` | left speed | m/s |
| `omega` | counter-clockwise turn rate | rad/s |

**Kinematics** is the geometry that turns that one request into four `SwerveModuleState`s: a speed and an angle for each wheel.
You never compute those by hand. The CTRE drivetrain, generated for the team's robot, does it every loop.

## What the wheels do

Wheel positions are about 0.283 m from the center in each direction (11.125 in on the team robot), so each wheel is about 0.40 m from the center.
Angles are in the robot frame: 0° forward, counter-clockwise positive.

| Request | Every wheel | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Forward: `vx` = 2 | 2.0 m/s at 0° | All wheels agree |
| Strafe left: `vy` = 2 | 2.0 m/s at 90° | Wheels all turn sideways |
| Spin: `omega` = 1 | 0.40 m/s each. FL 135°, FR 45°, BL 225°, BR −45° | Each wheel is tangent to a circle around the center |
| Forward plus spin: `vx` = 2, `omega` = 1 | FL 1.74 m/s at 9.4°, FR 2.30 m/s at 7.1°, BL 1.74 m/s at −9.4°, BR 2.30 m/s at −7.1° | Spin is added to forward; the right side speeds up, the left side slows |

Each wheel's motion is just the robot's translation plus the spin at that corner, added as vectors.

<GoDeeper title="why a wheel may show a speed with a flipped angle">
Turning a wheel 180° takes time. Instead, the code can point it at the angle within 90° of where it is now and run it backward.
So you may see a state such as 45° at negative speed instead of 225° at positive speed. The wheel moves the same way on the floor.
</GoDeeper>

## Checkpoint

<Checkpoint lesson="6.2">

<details>
<summary>What does your code ask for, and what does the drivetrain turn it into?</summary>
Your code asks for a `ChassisSpeeds` (vx, vy, omega) for the whole robot. Kinematics turns it into a `SwerveModuleState` (speed and angle) for each of the four wheels.
</details>

<details>
<summary>In a pure spin, why are the four wheel angles all different?</summary>
Each wheel must move tangent to a circle around the robot's center, and the tangent direction differs at each corner.
</details>

</Checkpoint>

## Recap

<RecapCard>
- A module is a drive motor, a steer motor, and a CANcoder for the wheel angle.
- You ask for `ChassisSpeeds` (vx, vy, omega); kinematics produces four `SwerveModuleState`s.
- Forward and strafe point all wheels the same way; spin points them tangent to a circle.
- The CTRE drivetrain does the math, so you never compute module states by hand.
</RecapCard>
