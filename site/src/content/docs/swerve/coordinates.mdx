<WhyItMatters>
Nearly every swerve bug is a sign error: the robot spins the wrong way, drives backward, or only works on one alliance.
Before you write any driving code, you need to know which way is +X, which way is +Y, and which way counts as a positive turn.
</WhyItMatters>

## The field frame

WPILib puts one fixed coordinate system on the field. Every pose in your code, and every target such as a scoring spot, is written in it.

- The **origin** (0, 0) is the corner at the right end of the blue alliance wall, as seen by a blue driver looking out at the field.
- **+X** points away from the blue wall, toward the red wall.
- **+Y** points to the left, as seen from the blue driver station.
- A **heading** of 0 faces +X. Angles are **counter-clockwise positive**.

Distances are in meters. Angles are in radians in code, but a `Rotation2d` holds the angle for you and lets you read it as degrees or radians (`Rotation2d.fromDegrees(90)`, `getRadians()`).

| Pose (x m, y m, heading) | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| (0, 0, 0°) | In the origin corner, facing the red wall |
| (2, 1, 90°) | 2 m from the blue wall, 1 m left of the origin side, facing left (+Y) |
| (4.625, 4.035, 0°) | The team's blue hub position, facing the red wall |
| (11.915, 4.035, 180°) | The team's red hub position (`16.54 - 4.625`), facing the blue wall |
| (3, 2, −90°) | Facing right (−Y), back toward the origin side wall |

## The robot frame

The robot has its own axes that move with it. Speeds you ask for are written in this frame.

| Motion | Sign |
| --- | --- |
| `vx` forward | positive |
| `vx` backward | negative |
| `vy` strafe left | positive |
| `vy` strafe right | negative |
| `omega` turn counter-clockwise (left) | positive |
| `omega` turn clockwise (right) | negative |

The robot frame and the field frame match only when the robot faces +X (heading 0).
Turn the robot to 90° and robot-forward becomes field +Y.

## Alliance flipping

The field frame never changes with the alliance. The blue origin is always at the blue wall, even when you are red.
A red driver pushes the stick "forward", away from themselves, and that means toward the blue wall: **negative X** on the field.

Code has two ways to handle this. Either flip the driver's view (rotate stick input by 180° on red), or mirror each target for the red side.
The team's drivetrain does the first: it sets an **operator perspective** whenever the robot is disabled.

<GoDeeper title="why the angle unit is a trap">
Java's `Math.sin` and `Math.cos` want radians, but people think in degrees. Passing 90 where 1.5708 is expected gives nonsense without any error.
Keep angles inside `Rotation2d` and only convert at the edges, for example when printing.
</GoDeeper>

## Recap

<RecapCard>
- Field frame: origin at the blue wall's right corner, +X toward red, +Y left, counter-clockwise positive.
- Robot frame: +X forward, +Y left, counter-clockwise positive.
- Code uses meters and radians; `Rotation2d` hides the unit.
- The field never flips with the alliance, so code flips the driver's view or mirrors targets.
</RecapCard>
