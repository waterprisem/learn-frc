<WhyItMatters>
To score from the right spot or drive an autonomous path, the robot has to know where it is on the field.
No sensor says that directly, so the code builds an estimate called the **pose** and keeps it up to date every loop.
Every later lesson (vision, paths, aiming) starts from this number, so its errors become everyone's errors.
</WhyItMatters>

## What a pose is

A `Pose2d` is a position plus a heading, in the field frame from lesson 6.1.

| Part | Type | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| x | distance from the field origin along the field's long axis | meters |
| y | distance from the origin across the field | meters |
| heading | which way the robot faces, counter-clockwise positive | `Rotation2d` (read as degrees or radians) |

The pose is not the gyro heading. The gyro gives one number (the heading). The pose adds where you are.

## Odometry: adding up movement

**Odometry** starts from a known pose and, many times a second, adds the small movement measured since the last update.
Each wheel's drive encoder says how far it rolled and its steer angle says which way. The gyro supplies the robot's heading.
The generated drivetrain does this on its own fast loop, so you never write the update.

Odometry is only as good as its inputs, so errors **accumulate**: each update adds a tiny mistake and nothing removes it.

## Why the pose drifts

- **Wheel slip**: a wheel spins or skids, so the encoder reports distance that was not covered.
- **Collisions and pushing**: the robot moves without the wheels turning the way the code expects.
- **Gyro error**: a heading that is 1 degree off sends every later meter slightly sideways.

A worked example: the gyro heading is 1 degree off, and the robot drives straight.

| Distance driven | Sideways error (distance times sin 1 degree) |
| --- | --- |
| 5 m | 0.09 m |
| 10 m | 0.17 m |
| 20 m | 0.35 m |

That is one small error source. A match has several, so by the end the pose can be tens of centimeters wrong.
Vision (track 7) fixes this by reading AprilTags at known field spots. A **pose estimator** blends the two sources: it gives each a **standard deviation**, a number for how much to distrust it (small means trusted).
A tag measured from close up gets a small value and pulls the pose strongly. A noisy one barely moves it.

<GoDeeper title="why the estimator is called a Kalman filter">
WPILib's swerve pose estimator and CTRE's drivetrain both use a Kalman filter: a standard way to blend noisy measurements by their standard deviations.
You do not need the math yet. The idea is a weighted average where the trusted source gets the larger weight.
</GoDeeper>

## Recap

<RecapCard>
- A `Pose2d` is x and y in meters plus a heading, in the field frame.
- Odometry adds up wheel movement and gyro heading, so small errors accumulate.
- Slip, collisions and gyro error cause drift; vision corrects it.
- A pose estimator blends sources using standard deviations: smaller means more trust.
- Read the pose with `drivetrain.getState().Pose`; `SwerveTelemetry` publishes it for AdvantageScope.
</RecapCard>
