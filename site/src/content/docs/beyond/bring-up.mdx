<WhyItMatters>
Code that works in simulation has never met a flipped motor, a miswired encoder, or a gear ratio typed wrong.
On a real mechanism those mistakes mean a shooter wheel spinning backward or an arm driving into its hard stop at full voltage.
Bring-up is the short, fixed routine that finds each of them while the power is still small.
</WhyItMatters>

## The first-enable checklist

Do the steps in order. Each one makes the next one safe.

1. **Every device shows up in Phoenix Tuner X** with the right CAN ID and current firmware (lessons 1.5 and R.6). A missing or duplicate ID is cheaper to fix now than to debug as a "dead mechanism" later.
2. **Put the robot on blocks, free the mechanism, clear the people.** The first command may spin the wrong way. Nobody's hands go near anything that moves (lesson 1.14).
3. **Set current limits low** (lesson 1.3). If something jams, the motor stalls at a small current instead of cooking itself or browning out the robot.
4. **Confirm the neutral mode** (brake or coast). It decides what the mechanism does when you stop driving it, which matters for an arm that must not fall.
5. **Apply a small positive voltage** (about 1 V) and watch the motion. It must move in the direction your code calls positive. If not, fix the motor's inversion in configuration, not with a minus sign in a command.
6. **Move it in the positive direction by hand or with voltage and read the sensor.** The reading must count up. If it counts down, the sensor direction is wrong, and any feedback loop would push the wrong way.
7. **Check the conversion.** Move a known distance or angle (10 inches, a quarter turn) and compare the reading. A wrong gear ratio or wheel size shows up here as a number that is off by a constant factor.
8. **Set soft limits** so the code refuses to drive past the safe range.
9. **Home** the mechanism (lesson 1.6) so the sensor zero means something physical.
10. **Only now close the loop**: low gains, low target, one hand near the disable key.
11. **Watch the Driver Station** the whole time for CAN errors and brownouts (lesson R.4).

<GoDeeper title="why inversion belongs in configuration">
If you flip the direction in the motor configuration, every command and the sensor stay consistent: positive voltage means positive motion everywhere.
If you patch it with a minus sign in one command, the next command, the logging, and the simulation each need the same patch, and someone will forget one.
</GoDeeper>

## What you see and what it means

| What you see | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| Device missing in Tuner X | Bad CAN wiring or connector, duplicate or wrong ID, no power |
| Mechanism moves the wrong way for a positive voltage | Motor inversion is wrong |
| Positive voltage works, but a position loop runs away at full output | Sensor counts the wrong way (sensor phase) |
| Reading is off by the same factor every time | Wrong conversion factor (gear ratio, wheel or spool size) |
| Motor twitches or does nothing, no movement at all | Current limit too low, breaker tripped, or the mechanism is binding |
| Robot browns out when the mechanism starts | Current limits too high, weak battery, or a loose power connection |
| CAN errors that come and go | Loose connector, missing terminating resistor, too many devices on one bus |
| Mechanism drifts after you stop driving it | Neutral mode is coast when you wanted brake |

## Checkpoint

<Checkpoint lesson="9.1">

<details>
<summary>Why check the direction with a small voltage before any PID?</summary>
A closed loop on a flipped motor or sensor pushes away from the target and gets stronger as the error grows. A small open-loop voltage shows the problem with nothing at risk.
</details>

<details>
<summary>The reading is exactly half of what you moved. What do you fix?</summary>
The conversion factor, such as the gear ratio. Direction and wiring are not the problem, because the reading moves the right way by a constant factor.
</details>

</Checkpoint>

## Recap

<RecapCard>
- Check devices, then safety, then limits, then direction, then sensor, then conversion, then loops.
- Fix motor and sensor direction in configuration.
- Use a known distance to verify the conversion.
- Soft limits and homing come before any closed loop.
- Keep the Driver Station open for CAN errors and brownouts.
</RecapCard>
